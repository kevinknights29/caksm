\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This thesis studied communication avoidance in a Krylov exponential integrator for two three-asset option PDEs. The coordinates $\Rv$ and $\Rh$ identify opportunities for cache reuse and fewer reductions. Their unit lines organize the experiments; they are not universal performance crossovers.

The CPU results separate the two mechanisms. Reducing the synchronization count by 11--16$\times$ gives no wall-clock gain because reductions are not the limiting cost. Cache-blocked matrix powers also remain below their traffic opportunity because halo arithmetic and latency dominate. Direct placement of all eight production grids moves from horizontal-only at $n=25$, through neither mechanism at $n=30$--61, to vertical-only from $n=74$. The map identifies which mechanism is worth measuring without promising a speedup.

Numerical admissibility is empirical. The $u^{-1/2}$ guard matches 20 of 21 cases, but the available theorem for CholeskyQR2 covers only about $1.25\times10^4$ at the production dimension. A measured Krylov block reaches $6\times10^6$. The numerical claims therefore rely on the guard, successful factorization, measured orthogonality, and agreement with the independent referee over the tested range.

On one V100, plane streaming accepts roughly half the occupancy in exchange for fewer halo instructions and load sectors. The promoted dispatch improves the full solver by a 1.248$\times$ geometric mean on four selection cases, with a 1.204$\times$ worst case. Four active held-out cases give a 1.239$\times$ mean and 1.182$\times$ worst case, while four small-grid fallback controls remain near parity at 0.991$\times$.

In the distributed solver, the equal-work $s=4$ arm is 0.98--1.01$\times$ the $s=1$ control on one V100 and 1.78--1.80$\times$ faster on four V100s. However, same-width strong scaling is only $0.89\times$, or about 22\% efficiency, and the best four-GPU time is 12--15\% slower than the best one-GPU time. The four measured topology rungs do not establish a scaling law. Communication also overlaps device work, so the cost decomposition is not additive. Peer-halo overlap hides 99.7\% of copy time but gives no end-to-end improvement within the measured ranges.

The largest-common-grid test stops at its independent boundary gate. At $n=337$, the Krylov residual passes, but the augmentation-tail error is $6.40\times10^{-5}$. No validated large-grid price is claimed.

For the stated Basket and Rainbow contracts, both the Krylov and smoothed Hundsdorfer--Verwer ADI solvers show second-order spatial refinement toward independent references and are compared at equal declared accuracy. Basket Delta errors are 0.011--0.020\%, and the node-stencil diagonal Gammas are consistent with the references within their 0.05--0.22\% uncertainty. Rainbow Delta and Gamma center differences are 0.17--0.24\% and 0.54--0.88\%, respectively. Off-node Gamma remains limited by extraction. These results apply to the tested three-asset European contracts and do not compare production pricing systems.

The main conclusion is methodological. Communication-avoiding performance depends on the operator, numerical basis, kernel geometry, topology, and a fair equal-work control. The regime coordinates organize these factors and show where richer models of tile redundancy and distributed synchronization are still needed.
